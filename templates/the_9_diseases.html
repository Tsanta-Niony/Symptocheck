Asthma is a chronic lung condition that affects people of all ages. It is caused by inflammation and contraction of the muscles around the airways, making breathing more difficult.



Symptoms:

  • Shortness of breath:difficulty breathing, especially during physical activity.

  • Wheezing:a high-pitched whistling sound while breathing, often more noticeable when exhaling.

  • Coughing:often persistent, especially at night or early in the morning.

  • Chest tightness:a feeling of pressure or pain in the chest.

  • Fatigue:due to the extra effort required to breathe.


Treatments:


      I) Long-term control medications


    • Inhaled corticosteroids: used regularly to reduce airway inflammation (e.g., budesonide, fluticasone).

    • Long-acting beta-agonists: often combined with corticosteroids to help prevent attacks (e.g., salmeterol,formoterol).

    • Leukotriene modifiers: oral medications to reduce symptoms (e.g.,montelukast).


      II) Rescue (or quick-relief) medications


    • Short-acting beta-agonists: used for quick-relief of symptoms during an asthma attack (e.g., albuterol).

    • Anticholinergics:sometimes used to open the airways (e.g., ipratropium).


      III) Trigger management


    • Identify and avoid common triggers such as allergens (pollen, mold), smoke, cold air, and infections.

    • Maintain good household hygiene to reduce allergens in the home.


      IV) Asthma action plan


    • work with a healthcare professional to create a personalized plan to manage symptoms, prevent attacks, and know when to seek medical help for severe symptoms.


      V) Lifestyle measures


    • Engage in controlled and appropriate physical activity to strengthen the lungs.

    • Adopt a healthy diet and avoid smoking.
